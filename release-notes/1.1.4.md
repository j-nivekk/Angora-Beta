# Angora 1.1.4-beta.1

Thank you for testing Angora. This is the largest feature release since 1.1.0, and it is about what happens to a comment after you write it. Comments now announce themselves in the margin as you read, travel out of the app as clean Markdown, and carry emphasis you can type. Your colours become portable, the annotation list gains a keyboard, and Angora is prepared for macOS 27.

## At a glance

- **Comments you can see** — a coloured dot in the margin beside every marked-up passage that carries a comment. Hover to fan out a cluster, click to open one.
- **The margin follows your filter** — narrow the annotation list and the dots narrow with it, dimming everything out of focus rather than hiding it.
- **Export annotations as Markdown** — the whole document or exactly what your filter shows, as a file or straight to the clipboard.
- **Copy as Clean Text (⌘⇧C)** — PDF text without the broken line endings, stray hyphens and stubborn ligatures. Plain ⌘C is untouched.
- **⌘B and ⌘I in comments** — Markdown emphasis where your cursor already is.
- **⌥↑ and ⌥↓** — step through annotations without leaving the page.
- **Your palette, portable** — save your tool and Quick Colours as a code or a file, and restore them on another Mac.
- **A calmer colour filter** — near-identical colours now cluster into a single chip instead of crowding the row.
- **A Colours tab in Settings** — everything about colour in one place, with tabs that scroll when the window is small.
- **Ready for macOS 27** — the system's own comment bubble is suppressed, and note-box text stays sharp at any zoom.

## Comments you can see

A comment used to be invisible until you opened the sidebar. Now every highlight, underline and strike-out that carries a comment shows a small dot in the margin, tinted to the annotation's own colour — a cue in your peripheral vision, and a doorway when you want it.

- **One dot per comment, clustered by line.** Up to three comments on the same line sit as a neat overlapping stack; beyond that they collapse to a single dot with a count.
- **Hover to fan.** Move the pointer onto a cluster and it blooms open with an eased, staggered animation, then folds away as soon as you leave.
- **Click to read and edit.** A click opens that comment for editing and returns your previous tool afterwards, so the margin never costs you your place.
- **The filter is the controller.** The dots mirror the annotation list's current filter, search and sort all at once. Anything outside the current focus fades to a faint, non-interactive layer — still there for context, no longer competing for attention. With the list closed or unfiltered, every dot shows at full strength.
- **Tuned to taste.** **Settings → Annotation → Comments** offers **Margin**, **Size** and **Opacity**, with a live preview and **Reset to Defaults**. Turn the indicators off entirely if you prefer a bare page.

## Taking your annotations with you

- **Export Annotations as Markdown.** Under **File**, export **All…** or **Currently Filtered…**. Each entry quotes the marked passage as a blockquote with your comment beneath it and a `Page · Colour · Type` tag, grouped by page.
- **What you see is what you export.** The filtered option writes exactly the set the sidebar is showing, so a colour, a search or a type becomes an export scope without any further work.
- **Or copy it.** The same Markdown can go straight to the clipboard for a note in another app.

## Copying text that behaves

PDF text arrives broken: wrapped mid-sentence, hyphenated across lines, littered with ligatures that other apps render as tofu. **Copy as Clean Text (⌘⇧C)** repairs it on the way out.

- **Wrapped lines join into paragraphs,** while real paragraph breaks survive.
- **Hyphenation at line breaks is removed,** so *anno-\nation* becomes *annotation*.
- **Ligatures are normalised** (ﬁ → fi), and curly quotes can be straightened.
- **Each step is optional,** under **Settings → Annotation → Copying Text**.
- **Plain ⌘C is unchanged** and still copies exactly what the PDF contains.

## Writing comments

- **⌘B and ⌘I now work in comment fields.** They toggle Markdown emphasis around your selection — or at the cursor, ready for what you type next.
- **The markers stay visible.** Angora writes plain Markdown rather than hiding it behind rich text, so your comments remain readable everywhere they end up, including the new Markdown export.
- **One keystroke, one undo.** Toggling emphasis is a single undo step.

## Moving between annotations

- **⌥↑ and ⌥↓ step through your annotations,** advancing the sidebar selection and centring each one in the document with a brief flash.
- **It follows the list you are looking at,** traversing the current filtered and sorted order rather than raw document order — and falling back to document order when nothing is filtered.
- **It stays out of your way.** While a comment editor has focus the shortcuts yield to it, and stepping stops at the first and last annotation rather than wrapping.

## Your colours, portable

- **Save your palette.** **Settings → Colours → Backup & Share** turns your six tool colours, nine Quick Colours and any names you have given them into a short code you can paste on another Mac — or a file you can keep.
- **See before you apply.** Importing shows a swatch preview and tells you how many colour names travelled with it, so nothing is replaced sight unseen.
- **Only your defaults change.** Documents you already have open keep their own colour choices.

## A calmer colour filter

- **Near-identical colours now cluster.** Two colours that differ only by rounding or colour space used to occupy two filter chips; a perceptual comparison now merges them into one, and the row stays legible on a heavily annotated document.
- **One definition of a colour.** The two places that decided what counts as "the same colour" have been consolidated into a single one, so the filter, the list and the margin dots always agree.

## Settings, reorganised

- **A new Colours tab** gathers default colours, Quick Colours, presets and palette sharing in one place.
- **Tabs scroll** when the window is too narrow to show them all, instead of compressing into illegibility.
- **Sliders move smoothly.** Every settings slider now follows the pointer rather than snapping between notches, while values shown as whole numbers stay whole.

## Ready for macOS 27

macOS 27 changes how PDFKit presents annotations. Angora meets it on both counts.

- **No competing comment bubble.** macOS 27 attaches its own bubble to marked-up text that carries a comment, opening its own editor. Angora suppresses it — the margin indicators answer the same question in the same strip of page, and two affordances competing for one job helps nobody.
- **Note boxes stay sharp.** On macOS 27 annotation content was drawn at a fixed resolution and then scaled, leaving note-box text soft on a Retina display and visibly coarse as you zoomed in. Angora now redraws it at your display's true resolution, at any zoom.

Both changes apply only on macOS 27; earlier systems are untouched.

## Refinements

- **Reordering, fixed on macOS 14 and 15.** Dragging the annotation list's sort tabs and colour filter chips now works on older macOS, not only on macOS 26 and later.
- **A steadier window subtitle,** derived directly from the document's page state instead of being kept in sync by hand.
- **Broader localisation,** covering the new comment-indicator controls across all five supported languages.

## Updating

If you are already running Angora, choose **Check for Updates…** and Sparkle will offer 1.1.4 (Smoke Pearl). Otherwise, download the latest `.dmg` from the [Releases page](https://github.com/j-nivekk/Angora-Beta/releases), open it, and drag Angora into your Applications folder.

The Beta is signed with an Apple Development certificate but is **not notarised by Apple**, so macOS Gatekeeper will block it on first launch. You only need to do this once:

- **Recommended.** Control-click **Angora Beta** in Applications → **Open** → **Open**.
- Or, after a blocked attempt, open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**.
- Or, from Terminal: `xattr -cr /Applications/Angora\ Beta.app`.

## System requirements

- macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later
- Apple Silicon or Intel
